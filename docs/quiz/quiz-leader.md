# BC AI Workshop — Comprehension Quiz (Leader Deck)

> **For event organizer use.** Ten multiple-choice questions, in leader-deck order.
> Each question has exactly one correct answer. The answer key is at the bottom of this file.
> Recommended time: ~12 minutes (≈70 seconds per question).

> **Section letters are the LEADER deck's, not the standard deck's.** The leader deck runs four
> leader-only sections — **B** THE GAP, **C** THE SHAPE, **D** WHY INVEST and **K** THE MANDATE —
> and drops the TECHNIQUES section. Every retained curriculum section therefore sits under a
> different letter than in `quiz.md`: mindset is **F** (not C), process **G** (not D),
> fundamentals **H** (not E), tools **I** (not G), pitfalls **J** (not H), principles **M** (not J).
>
> **Four questions are new** (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q9 — sections B, C, D, K). **Six are retained** from
> `quiz.md` with only the section letter updated. All four new questions use content that is the
> same in every brand variant of the deck (berau / gems / general).

## Section B — The Gap

### Q1. One Signature, Two Clocks

Section B opened by starting **two clocks from one signature**: a **Tool access** lane (models,
licences, tools) tagged **PROCURED**, and an **Organizational capability** lane (decision rights,
workflow, skills, incentives, measurement) tagged **EARNED**. Which statement matches the slide's
reading of that race?

- **A.** Both lanes close on day 1 — the signature is what closes the gap.
- **B.** Tool access is done on day 1, for everyone at once; capability is still running, one changed habit at a time — and it never arrives on an invoice.
- **C.** Capability moves first and fast; slow procurement is the real bottleneck the section warns about.
- **D.** Neither lane starts until a separate training budget has been approved.

## Section C — The Shape

### Q2. Where Adoption Actually Spreads From

Section C argued that adoption is **not top-down** — it spreads out from one layer, because that
layer is the only one holding both halves of what a change needs. Which layer, and why?

- **A.** Top management — it holds the mandate and the budget, so it can order the change directly.
- **B.** Middle management — near enough to the work to see it, senior enough to change it, and the one layer the teams copy because they watch it work.
- **C.** The teams — they hold the deepest knowledge of the work, the exceptions and every workaround, so adoption starts and ends with them.
- **D.** Whichever layer has the largest headcount — adoption is a numbers game.

## Section D — Why Invest

### Q3. The Surface and the Source

Section D put two identical-looking charts side by side. One was built by **a single prompt in
thirty minutes**. The other had **five days** under it that nobody sees — the source of each
number, what each field means, the match between two systems, and a person who checked it against
the real thing. What was the section's point?

- **A.** The picture is cheap now and the work under it is not — you are buying the work, not the picture, and the surface never tells you which chart is which.
- **B.** AI models still design badly, so a human designer is what the investment pays for.
- **C.** Once the model is good enough at charts, the five days of data work can be removed.
- **D.** A finished-looking result is evidence that the data under it was checked.

## Section F — Mindset

### Q4. The Shape of Human–AI Collaboration

Section F describes the shape of human–AI collaboration as a four-stage workflow:
**Specify → Generate → Verify → Ship It.** Which option best captures *who does what* in this new
shape of work?

- **A.** Humans specify, generate, and verify; AI only ships the result.
- **B.** Humans focus on **Specify** and **Verify**; AI handles the bulk of **Generate**.
- **C.** AI specifies and verifies; humans generate the actual content by hand.
- **D.** Humans and AI split every stage roughly 50/50.

## Section G — Process & Methodology

### Q5. Walking the Decision Tree

Section G presented a five-question decision tree for choosing the right level of automation
(**BPM → RPA → IPA → Agentic**). The tree must be walked **in order**, and skipping a level was
warned to make "the level above fail harder."

A team walks the tree and finds: their process *works today*, they have *already removed waste and
bottlenecks*, they have *repetitive rule-based steps*, BUT the steps **do not** need AI's core
strengths (summarization, NLU, etc.). Which level is the correct fit?

- **A.** BPM — go back and redesign the process.
- **B.** RPA — automate the rule-based steps; reclaim hours.
- **C.** IPA — layer AI's core strengths on top.
- **D.** Agentic — let an autonomous agent pursue the goal.

## Section H — Engineering Fundamentals

### Q6. The Three Nested Layers

Section H introduced three nested engineering layers of AI system design, where each outer layer
*contains* the inner ones. Which option lists them correctly from **innermost (the instructions)**
to **outermost (the system around the model)**?

- **A.** Prompt → Context → Harness
- **B.** Context → Prompt → Harness
- **C.** Harness → Context → Prompt
- **D.** Prompt → Harness → Context

## Section I — Tools Ecosystem

### Q7. The Specialization Principle

Section I surveyed Claude, Google, and OpenAI ecosystems and closed with a guiding principle for
choosing among them. Which statement matches the principle as presented?

- **A.** "Pick the most capable vendor and use it for everything."
- **B.** "Open-source tools always beat frontier ones in the long run."
- **C.** "Pick the right tool for each task — never the right vendor for every task."
- **D.** "Always default to the cheapest tool that gets the job done."

## Section J — Pitfalls & Best Practices

### Q8. Untrained Use vs. Trained Use

Section J opened with **eight pitfalls of untrained AI use** — things like *vibe coding*,
*prompt-and-pray*, *tool overload*, *context rot*, and *confidently-wrong*. Which of the following
was **NOT** listed as one of those untrained-use pitfalls?

- **A.** Vibe coding
- **B.** Prompt-and-pray
- **C.** Context rot
- **D.** Spec-driven iteration

## Section K — The Mandate

### Q9. The Four Levers

Section K closed the leader's ask with **four levers** — *speak at the start*, *back the
Ambassadors*, *open the access*, *keep it funded*. What do all four have in common?

- **A.** Each one needs sign-off from IT, Finance and HR before it can start.
- **B.** Each one sits inside the leader's own authority — all four need the same signature, and nobody else has to agree.
- **C.** Each one needs a new budget line approved at group level first.
- **D.** Each one is delegated to the AI Ambassadors, who own the decision.

## Section M — Principles (Closing)

### Q10. Mindset Before Tools

Section M closed the workshop with **five hard-earned mindset principles**, framed as
*"Mindset before tools."* Which of the following is one of those five principles as it appeared on
the slide?

- **A.** "Foundation precedes velocity — understand before you output."
- **B.** "Always adopt the newest model the day it launches."
- **C.** "Optimize for output first; refine the spec later."
- **D.** "Solo builders should skip specs and ship by feel."

- **9–10 correct** — Strong grasp of both the leader argument and the curriculum; ready to decide.

- **3–5 correct** — Core ideas landed; recommend a recap of Section B (the gap) and Section K (the four levers).
- **0–2 correct** — Encourage a re-watch; focus first on Sections B, C and D (gap, shape, case) before the curriculum.
